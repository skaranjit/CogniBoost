# Spatial Reasoning Games Specifications

This document outlines the specifications for the "Spatial Reasoning Games" category within the CogniBoost application. These games are designed to enhance a player's ability to visualize, manipulate, and reason about spatial relationships, shapes, and movements in two-dimensional (2D) and three-dimensional (3D) space.

## 1. Masterpiece

*   **Game Name:** Masterpiece
*   **Core Concept:** This game focuses on assembling 2D pieces to perfectly match a target image or fill a specific outline. It emphasizes the mental manipulation of shapes, understanding how they fit together, and visualizing the completed picture from its components. While it involves problem-solving, its core cognitive challenge lies in spatial visualization and pattern completion.
*   **Cognitive Skills Targeted:**
    *   Primary: Spatial Visualization, Pattern Recognition, Mental Rotation (of 2D shapes), Form Constancy.
    *   Secondary: Problem-Solving, Attention to Detail, Fine Motor Skills (drag-and-drop), Visual Closure.
*   **Gameplay Mechanics:**
    *   **Interface:** A workspace displaying a target outline or a faint background image, alongside a collection of available 2D puzzle pieces (e.g., mosaic tiles, jigsaw pieces, or abstract geometric shapes).
    *   **Interaction:** Players select pieces and then drag, drop, and rotate (and possibly flip, depending on the game variant) them to fit correctly within the target area.
    *   **Feedback:** Pieces might "snap" into place when correctly positioned, or provide other visual/auditory cues. Incorrect placements might be disallowed or highlighted.
*   **Customizable Difficulty:**
    *   **Number of Pieces:** More pieces significantly increase complexity.
    *   **Complexity of Shapes:** Intricate, non-standard, or highly similar shapes are harder to place.
    *   **Similarity of Pieces:** Pieces that look alike but have subtle differences (e.g., in color or edge detail).
    *   **Obscurity of Target Outline:** A vague or absent outline makes it more challenging than a clear template.
    *   **Time Limits:** Optional time pressure for completion.
    *   **Hints:** Options to show the correct placement or orientation for one or more pieces, or to reveal parts of the completed image.
    *   **Rotation/Flipping:** Games that require rotation are harder than those with fixed orientations. Adding flipping increases difficulty further.
*   **Success Metrics:**
    *   Time to completion.
    *   Number of moves (drags, drops) and rotations/flips.
    *   Accuracy of fit (e.g., no overlaps, all gaps correctly filled).
    *   Percentage of image completed (if applicable).
    *   Use of hints.

## 2. Clockwork Brain (General Type) / Mechanical Puzzles

*   **Game Name/Category:** Clockwork Brain / Mechanical Puzzles
*   **Core Concept:** This category includes games that involve understanding, visualizing, and mentally simulating the interaction of mechanical parts such as gears, levers, belts, or paths for objects. Players typically need to assemble, adjust, or predict the outcome of a given mechanical system to achieve a specific goal. The theme emphasizes spatial relationships in a dynamic, cause-and-effect context.
*   **Cognitive Skills Targeted:**
    *   Primary: Spatial Reasoning (especially understanding 3D relationships if applicable), Mechanical Reasoning, Visualization of Cause and Effect, System Thinking.
    *   Secondary: Problem-Solving, Logical Thinking, Planning, Trial-and-Error.
*   **General Gameplay Mechanics:**
    *   **Interface:** Presents a system of interconnected (or to-be-connected) parts, often with a clear start and end point or a goal state.
    *   **Interaction:** Users might add components from an inventory, remove existing ones, rotate or adjust parts (e.g., change gear sizes, flip switches, draw paths), and then "run" or "activate" the system to observe the outcome.
    *   **Feedback:** Visual feedback on how the system operates, highlighting successful connections, motion transfer, or blockages.
*   **Examples of Variations:**
    *   **Gear Assembly:** Players are given a set of gears of different sizes and must arrange them to connect a driving gear to a target gear, often across a distance or around obstacles.
    *   **Path Puzzles (e.g., Pipe Dreams, Incredible Machine style):** Lay down track pieces, pipes, conveyor belts, or other guiding elements to create a continuous path for an object (ball, liquid, character) to reach a destination, possibly triggering other events along the way.
    *   **Physics-based Puzzles:** Arrange a set of static and dynamic objects (e.g., ramps, dominoes, catapults, balls) so that when physics is "turned on" (e.g., gravity takes effect, an initial force is applied), a desired chain reaction or outcome occurs.
    *   **Circuit Building:** Connect logical gates or electrical components to achieve a specific output.
*   **Customizable Difficulty:**
    *   **Number of Components:** More parts to place or consider.
    *   **Complexity of the System/Goal:** Requiring more intricate interactions or longer chains of cause and effect.
    *   **Constraints on Placement:** Limited space, fixed anchor points, or restrictions on which parts can connect.
    *   **Number of Possible Solutions:** Puzzles with one unique solution vs. multiple valid approaches.
    *   **Time Limits:** Optional pressure to solve quickly.
    *   **Availability of Parts:** Limited inventory of components.
*   **Success Metrics:**
    *   Successful completion of the mechanical goal (e.g., target gear spins, object reaches destination).
    *   Efficiency of the solution (e.g., fewest parts used, shortest path created, fastest completion of the action).
    *   Time taken to design and test the solution.
    *   Number of attempts or adjustments made.

## 3. Tangrams

*   **Game Name:** Tangrams
*   **Core Concept:** A classic dissection puzzle where the player uses seven flat polygons, called "tans" (a large triangle, a medium triangle, five small triangles, a square, and a parallelogram), to form a specific target silhouette. All seven pieces must be used, they must touch, and they cannot overlap.
*   **Cognitive Skills Targeted:**
    *   Primary: Spatial Visualization, Shape Recognition, Geometric Reasoning, Mental Rotation, Part-to-Whole Relationships.
    *   Secondary: Problem-Solving, Patience, Attention to Detail, Perceptual Reasoning.
*   **Gameplay Mechanics:**
    *   **Interface:** The seven standard tangram pieces are provided in a work area. A target silhouette (outline of a shape, e.g., an animal, person, or abstract design) is displayed.
    *   **Interaction:** Player drags, drops, and rotates each of the seven pieces to arrange them within the work area to precisely match the target silhouette.
    *   **Rules:** All seven pieces must be used. Pieces must be placed adjacent to each other (at least one point touching). No part of any piece may overlap another.
*   **Customizable Difficulty:**
    *   **Complexity of the Target Silhouette:** Silhouettes can range from simple, recognizable forms to complex and abstract designs that offer fewer cues about piece placement.
    *   **Provision of Hints:**
        *   Showing the outline of one or more pieces within the silhouette.
        *   Briefly flashing the solution.
        *   Allowing a "check fit" option for a piece.
    *   **Time Limits:** Optional time pressure.
    *   **Initial Orientation of Pieces:** Presenting pieces in orientations that require more mental rotation.
*   **Success Metrics:**
    *   Successful and accurate replication of the target silhouette.
    *   Time taken to complete the puzzle.
    *   Number of moves (drags, rotations).
    *   Use of hints.
    *   Efficiency (e.g., fewer "undo" actions or piece collisions).

This document will serve as a blueprint for the development of these spatial reasoning games, and can be further detailed with specific UI/UX designs and interaction flows.

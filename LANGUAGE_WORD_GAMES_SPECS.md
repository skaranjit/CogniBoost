# Language & Word Games Specifications

This document outlines the specifications for the "Language & Word Games" category within the CogniBoost application. These games are designed to enhance vocabulary, verbal fluency, reading comprehension, and other language-related cognitive skills.

## 1. Vocabulary Building Games

This sub-category includes games focused on expanding a user's lexicon, improving spelling, and enhancing the ability to recall and use words effectively.

### A. Word Bubbles

*   **Game Name:** Word Bubbles
*   **Core Concept:** Players form words by selecting letters that appear inside floating bubbles. Bubbles may rise or move across the screen, and players must quickly tap letters in sequence to form valid words before the bubbles disappear or time runs out.
*   **Cognitive Skills Targeted:**
    *   Primary: Vocabulary, Verbal Fluency, Spelling.
    *   Secondary: Processing Speed, Attention, Visual Scanning.
*   **Gameplay Mechanics:**
    *   **Interface:** A screen where letter-filled bubbles float upwards or around.
    *   **Interaction:** Player taps bubbles (letters) in sequence to spell a word. A "submit" button or gesture confirms the word.
    *   **Word Validation:** System checks if the formed word is valid (e.g., against a dictionary).
    *   **Scoring:** Points awarded based on word length, letter rarity (e.g., Scrabble-like scores), or complexity. Bonus points for using specific letters or forming words related to a theme (if applicable).
*   **Customizable Difficulty:**
    *   **Speed of Bubbles:** Faster moving or shorter-lived bubbles.
    *   **Rarity of Letters:** Adjusting the frequency of common vs. uncommon letters.
    *   **Minimum Word Length:** Requiring longer words for points.
    *   **Time Limits:** Overall game time limit or time limit per word.
    *   **Themed Word Lists:** Optional themes that guide word formation (e.g., "animals," "food").
    *   **Presence of "difficult" letters:** (Q, X, Z) appearing more or less frequently.
*   **Success Metrics:**
    *   Number of valid words formed.
    *   Total points scored.
    *   Longest word formed.
    *   Variety of words (e.g., number of unique words).
    *   Average word length.

### B. Contextual (or "Contextual Word Choice")

*   **Game Name:** Contextual (or Contextual Word Choice)
*   **Core Concept:** Players are presented with sentences or short paragraphs containing an incorrectly used word, an ambiguous word, or a blank space. They must identify the error or choose the most appropriate word from a set of options to ensure the sentence is grammatically correct and contextually meaningful.
*   **Cognitive Skills Targeted:**
    *   Primary: Vocabulary, Reading Comprehension, Grammar, Contextual Understanding.
    *   Secondary: Critical Thinking, Analytical Skills, Attention to Detail.
*   **Gameplay Mechanics:**
    *   **Interface:** Text passages are displayed.
    *   **Interaction:**
        *   A specific word might be highlighted, and the player decides if it's correct or incorrect. If incorrect, they choose the right word from multiple choices.
        *   A blank space is provided, and the player selects the best fit from multiple choices.
    *   **Feedback:** Immediate feedback on whether the choice was correct, possibly with a brief explanation.
*   **Customizable Difficulty:**
    *   **Subtlety of Incorrect Word Usage:** Errors can be obvious (e.g., wrong part of speech) or very subtle (e.g., nuanced meaning differences between synonyms).
    *   **Complexity of Sentence Structure:** Longer sentences, more complex grammar.
    *   **Obscurity of Vocabulary:** Using less common words in the passage and options.
    *   **Number of Choices:** More options to choose from can increase difficulty.
*   **Success Metrics:**
    *   Accuracy of word choices (percentage correct).
    *   Time taken per question or for a set of questions.
    *   Number of correct identifications.
    *   Level progression based on consistent accuracy.

### C. Word Snatchers

*   **Game Name:** Word Snatchers
*   **Core Concept:** A fast-paced game where players quickly form words from a shared or dynamically appearing pool of letters. The "snatching" aspect implies competition, either against a timer or an AI opponent, to find and claim words first.
*   **Cognitive Skills Targeted:**
    *   Primary: Vocabulary, Verbal Fluency, Processing Speed, Spelling.
    *   Secondary: Strategic Thinking (e.g., using common letters quickly vs. forming longer, high-scoring words), Attention.
*   **Gameplay Mechanics:**
    *   **Interface:** A grid of letter tiles, a stream of falling letters, or a central pool of available letters.
    *   **Interaction:** Players tap or drag letters to form words. Word submission is quick.
    *   **Competition:**
        *   Against Timer: Form as many words as possible within a time limit.
        *   Against AI: AI opponent also forms words from the same pool; letters used are removed.
    *   **Scoring:** Points for each valid word, typically based on length and letter values. Bonus for speed or using specific letters.
*   **Customizable Difficulty:**
    *   **Time Limits:** Shorter time limits for increased pressure.
    *   **Number/Refresh Rate of Available Letters:** Affects word-finding opportunities.
    *   **Opponent AI Level (if applicable):** AI speed and vocabulary.
    *   **Minimum Word Length:** Requiring longer words.
    *   **Letter Distribution:** More challenging letter combinations.
*   **Success Metrics:**
    *   Total score.
    *   Number of words formed.
    *   Speed of word formation (words per minute).
    *   Longest word formed.
    *   Performance against AI (win/loss ratio).

## 2. Reading Comprehension Games

*   **Category Name:** Reading Comprehension Games
*   **Core Concept:** These games are designed to improve a user's ability to understand, interpret, and retain information from written passages. Users read texts and then answer questions or perform tasks that demonstrate their comprehension of the material.
*   **Cognitive Skills Targeted:**
    *   Primary: Reading Comprehension, Memory (short-term and working), Critical Thinking, Analytical Skills.
    *   Secondary: Vocabulary, Attention to Detail, Inferential Reasoning.
*   **General Gameplay Mechanics:**
    *   **Passage Presentation:** Users are shown text passages of varying lengths and complexity (e.g., short stories, factual articles, instructions, dialogues).
    *   **Question/Task Types:** After reading, users are presented with:
        *   Multiple-choice questions.
        *   True/False statements.
        *   Sequencing tasks (ordering events from the passage).
        *   Identifying main ideas, supporting details, or author's purpose.
        *   Matching information.
    *   **Feedback:** Information on correct/incorrect answers, often with explanations for why an answer is correct, potentially highlighting relevant parts of the text.
*   **Examples of Variations:**
    *   **Main Idea Identification:** Read a paragraph or short text and select the sentence that best states the main idea.
    *   **Detail Recall:** Answer specific "who, what, when, where, why" questions about explicit information in the text.
    *   **Inferential Questions:** Answer questions that require understanding implied meanings, drawing conclusions, or making predictions based on the text.
    *   **Passage Summarization (Selection):** Choose the best summary of a passage from several options.
    *   **Vocabulary in Context:** Determine the meaning of a specific word as used in the passage.
    *   **Sequencing Events:** Arrange a list of events from the passage into their correct chronological order.
*   **Customizable Difficulty:**
    *   **Length and Complexity of Passages:** Shorter, simpler sentences and vocabulary for easier levels; longer, more complex sentences with advanced vocabulary and abstract concepts for harder levels.
    *   **Lexile Level or Readability Score:** Passages can be graded.
    *   **Difficulty of Questions:** Questions can range from direct recall to complex inference.
    *   **Abstractness of Concepts:** Concrete topics vs. abstract or philosophical ideas.
    *   **Time Limits:** Optional time limits for reading the passage and/or answering questions.
    *   **Number of Questions per Passage:** More questions might require more thorough understanding.
*   **Success Metrics:**
    *   Accuracy of answers (percentage of correctly answered questions).
    *   Time taken to read the passage and answer questions.
    *   Comprehension score (could be a composite of accuracy and speed).
    *   Performance on different types of questions (e.g., to identify strengths/weaknesses in main idea vs. detail recall).
    *   Level progression based on sustained performance.

This document will serve as a blueprint for the development of these language and word games, and can be further detailed with specific UI/UX designs, content libraries, and interaction flows.
